Mozambique is the closest beach holiday abroad you can reach by road from Gauteng. No flights, no visa for South Africans, and within a day you're eating prawns by the Indian Ocean. Our Mozambique group trip spends two nights in Maputo and two at the Bilene lagoon, and we do the driving. Here's what to expect, from the border crossing to the last sundowner.

## Mozambique at a glance

- **Trip length:** 4 nights, 5 days. Two nights in Maputo, two in Bilene.
- **Getting there:** by road from Johannesburg, about 550 km to Maputo through the Lebombo border. Transport is included on our trip.
- **Time difference:** none. Mozambique is on the same time as South Africa.
- **Visa:** none needed for South Africans for stays of up to 30 days.
- **Money:** Mozambican metical (MZN). Rand is accepted in many places in the south.
- **Language:** Portuguese. English is understood in tourist areas.
- **Our 2027 departures:** 12–16 February, 2–6 April, 10–14 September and 26–30 November. R9,200 per person sharing.

## The drive: Johannesburg to Maputo

We leave Johannesburg early and head east on the N4 toll road through Mpumalanga, past eMalahleni, Mbombela and Komatipoort, to the Lebombo border post. Across the border, on the Mozambican side at Ressano Garcia, it's under two hours to Maputo.

Allow most of the day. The drive itself is about 7 hours with stops, and the border can add anything from half an hour to a few hours, depending on the day. Long weekends and school holidays are the busiest.

You don't need to worry about car papers, insurance or the temporary import permit that drivers need in Mozambique. That's our job. Your job is to keep your passport within reach and enjoy the road.

### Crossing the Lebombo / Ressano Garcia border

The crossing has two steps. First you're stamped out of South Africa at Lebombo, then stamped into Mozambique at Ressano Garcia. A few tips that make it painless:

- Keep your passport on you, not packed in your suitcase.
- Don't carry anything for anyone else.
- Have a pen ready, in case there are forms to fill in.
- Be patient and polite. Queues move faster for groups that are ready.

## Two nights in Maputo

Maputo is Mozambique's capital: a city of wide, tree-lined avenues, Portuguese-era buildings, live music and some of the best seafood in Africa. It wakes up late and stays up later.

Our Maputo city tour takes you through the highlights. Places you'll likely hear about:

- **Maputo Central Railway Station,** one of the most photographed railway stations in Africa, with its green dome and wrought-iron details.
- **The Fortress of Maputo (Fortaleza),** a 19th-century fort in the old town.
- **The Central Market (Mercado Central),** for cashews, spices, capulanas (bright printed cloth) and crafts.
- **The Maputo–Katembe Bridge,** which was the longest suspension bridge in Africa when it opened in 2018.
- **The Marginal and Costa do Sol,** the beachfront road for sunsets, sea air and seafood.

The South Beach experience is included too, so you'll get your first proper beach time in the city. And if you'd like to add the **Fish Market** (Mercado do Peixe), it's a Maputo ritual: choose your prawns, crayfish or fish at the market stalls and have them grilled at a restaurant next door.

## Two nights in Bilene

From Maputo we drive about three hours north to Praia do Bilene. Bilene sits on the Uembje Lagoon, a large saltwater lagoon cut off from the Indian Ocean by tall dunes. The water is warm, calm and shallow, so it's perfect for swimming, floating and watersports, even if you're not a strong swimmer.

The boat cruise to Coco Island is included. Optional extras in Bilene:

- Canoeing on the lagoon
- Speed tubing
- Jet skiing
- Beach quad biking
- Horse riding on the beach

Evenings in Bilene are slow: sundowners by the water, grilled fish and peri-peri chicken, and music from the beach bars.

## Do South Africans need a visa for Mozambique?

No. South African passport holders can visit Mozambique without a visa for up to 30 days per visit. What you do need:

- **A valid passport.** Official guidance asks for at least 30 days of validity after your return, but some border officials and airlines go by 6 months. Make it 6 months to be safe.
- **Two blank pages** for the entry and exit stamps.
- **Documents for children.** South African children need their own passports, and should travel with an unabridged birth certificate. If a child is travelling with only one parent, bring a consent affidavit from the other parent too.

Our [passport checklist for South Africans](/travel-talk/passport-checklist/) goes through all of this in more detail.

## Is there malaria in Mozambique?

Yes. Mozambique, including Maputo and Bilene, is a malaria area, and the risk is highest in the hot, rainy months from about November to April. Speak to a travel clinic or your GP a few weeks before you travel about whether you should take antimalarial tablets. In South Africa, the common options include doxycycline and atovaquone-proguanil, but a health professional should advise what's right for you.

Whatever you decide, avoid being bitten:

- Use an insect repellent containing DEET, especially from dusk.
- Wear long sleeves and long pants in the evenings.
- Sleep under a net or in a room with screens or air conditioning.

If you get a fever or flu-like symptoms in the weeks after you get home, see a doctor and tell them you've been in a malaria area.

## Money in Mozambique: meticais or rand?

- **Rand** is widely accepted in southern Mozambique, especially in tourist spots, but you'll usually get a poorer exchange rate, and your change in meticais.
- **Meticais** get you better prices at markets, small restaurants and beach bars. Draw them from bank ATMs in Maputo.
- **Cards** work in many Maputo restaurants and hotels, less so in Bilene. Carry cash for the lagoon days.
- **Tipping** around 10% at restaurants is appreciated.

## What to eat and drink in Mozambique

Mozambique's food is a mix of African, Portuguese and Indian Ocean flavours, and the seafood is the star.

- **Prawns (camarão),** grilled with garlic, lemon and peri-peri.
- **Frango à cafreal,** chargrilled chicken with a spicy, citrusy marinade.
- **Matapa,** cassava leaves cooked with peanuts and coconut milk.
- **Prego roll,** steak in a Portuguese bread roll.
- **Pão,** the soft Portuguese bread rolls you'll find everywhere.
- **2M and Laurentina,** the local beers. Or try an R&R: local rum with raspberry soda.

## A few Portuguese words that help

- **Olá:** hello
- **Bom dia / Boa tarde:** good morning / good afternoon
- **Obrigado (or Obrigada if you're a woman):** thank you
- **Por favor:** please
- **Quanto custa?:** how much is it?

## Which Mozambique departure suits you?

- **February:** hot, humid summer with afternoon storms. Warmest water of the year, and a Valentine's weekend by the sea.
- **April:** the rains are easing, days are warm and the evenings start to cool.
- **September:** dry, sunny and pleasant, around the mid-20s. Bring a light jacket for evenings.
- **November:** hot again before the December rush, with the start of the rainy season.

## What to pack for Mozambique

- Passport (plus copies on your phone)
- Swimwear, a cover-up and sunscreen
- Light clothes for the day, long sleeves for the evening
- Insect repellent with DEET
- Sandals and water shoes for the lagoon
- Cash in meticais and small rand notes
- A power bank, and a phone that's set up for roaming or a local SIM

## Mozambique questions we get asked

### How far is Mozambique from Johannesburg?

Maputo is about 550 km from Johannesburg by road, or about 7 hours of driving plus the border crossing. Bilene is about another 3 hours north of Maputo.

### What side of the road does Mozambique drive on?

The left, the same as South Africa.

### Can I use my South African phone in Mozambique?

Yes, but roaming can be expensive. Buy a roaming bundle before you leave, or a local SIM card in Maputo (you'll need your passport to register it).

### Is Mozambique safe for South Africans?

Southern Mozambique, including Maputo and Bilene, welcomes many South African visitors every year. Use the same care you would in any city: keep valuables out of sight, avoid quiet streets at night and use trusted transport.
