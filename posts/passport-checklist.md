Most travel stress starts with a document problem nobody spotted early. A passport that runs out too soon, no blank pages left, a child's affidavit that's missing. Each of these can stop you at the airport or the border, and none of them can be fixed in a hurry. Run this checklist on the day you pay your deposit, not the week you fly.

## 1. Is your passport valid for 6 months after you get home?

Many countries want your passport to be valid for at least six months beyond your travel dates. Indonesia (Bali), Thailand and the United Arab Emirates all ask for six months, and airlines can refuse to let you board if your passport doesn't meet the rule for your destination.

Some neighbouring countries ask for less, but six months is the safe rule for every trip. If your passport expires within six months of your return date, renew it before you do anything else.

## 2. Do you have enough blank pages?

Every border crossing uses space, because you're stamped out of one country and into the next. As a guide:

- **Keep at least two completely blank pages** for any international trip.
- **Multi-country trips need more.** Our [3 Countries trip](/trips/vicfalls/) crosses into Zimbabwe, Zambia and Botswana in one week, and each crossing adds stamps.
- **Even a weekend adds up.** A trip to Lesotho means four stamps: out of South Africa, into Lesotho, out of Lesotho and back into South Africa.

If you're down to your last few pages, consider renewing with a 48-page maxi passport.

## 3. Does your name match your booking exactly?

Flight tickets must match the name in your passport, letter for letter. If you've changed your surname since your passport was issued, or you use a nickname, tell us when you book. Fixing a ticket name close to departure can be costly, or impossible.

## 4. Is your passport in good condition?

Water damage, torn pages, a loose cover or a damaged photo page can get a passport refused at a border. If yours has been through the wash or is falling apart, replace it.

## How to renew your passport in South Africa

You can apply for or renew a South African passport at a Home Affairs office that issues passports, or at a bank branch that offers Home Affairs services. Bank branches are usually quicker and calmer, but you need to book and pay online through the Home Affairs eHomeAffairs system first.

### What to bring

- Your current passport (or your old one, if it has expired)
- Your green barcoded ID book or smart ID card
- Proof of payment, if you paid online

Your photo and fingerprints are taken when you apply, so you don't need passport photos at most offices.

### How much does a passport cost?

At the time of writing, a standard 32-page adult passport costs R600 when it's your first passport, your old one has expired or it has less than six months left. A 48-page maxi passport costs more, and so can renewing early, while your current passport still has more than six months left. Fees change, so check the current amounts on the [Home Affairs website](https://www.dha.gov.za/) before you go.

### How long does a passport take in South Africa?

Home Affairs aims to have passports ready in about two to three weeks, but in practice it can take longer, especially in busy seasons. Allow at least four to six weeks between applying and your trip. You'll get an SMS when your passport is ready to collect from the office or branch where you applied.

Adult passports are valid for 10 years, and children's passports for 5 years.

## Passports and documents for children

Every child needs their own passport, including babies. To apply for a child's passport, both parents usually need to give consent, either by being there or with the right affidavit. Bring the child's unabridged birth certificate.

When you travel across a border with children, South African rules still expect supporting documents:

- **Travelling with both parents:** carry the child's passport and an unabridged birth certificate that shows both parents' details.
- **Travelling with one parent:** add a parental consent affidavit from the other parent, no older than three months, plus a copy of their ID or passport and their contact details.
- **Travelling with another adult** (a grandparent or friend, for example): bring affidavits from both parents, copies of their IDs and their contact details.

## Beware of visa and levy scam sites

Searching for "Bali visa" or "Zanzibar insurance" brings up lookalike websites that charge two or three times the official price. Use only official government portals, which usually end in .go.id (Indonesia), .go.tz (Tanzania) or .go.th (Thailand), or ask us for the right link.

## Keep copies of everything

- Save a photo of your passport's photo page, and any visas, on your phone and in your email.
- Pack one printed copy separately from your passport.
- Share copies with someone you trust at home.
- Note the contact details of the South African embassy or high commission in the country you're visiting.

If your passport is lost or stolen abroad, report it to the local police and contact the nearest South African embassy or high commission for an emergency travel document.

## Your passport checklist, in one list

- Valid for 6 months after your return date
- At least 2 blank pages (more for multi-country trips)
- Name matches your booking exactly
- In good condition, with no water damage or torn pages
- Children have their own passports, birth certificates and any affidavits needed
- Visa, levy or insurance sorted for your destination, on official sites only
- Copies saved on your phone, in your email and on paper

## Passport questions we get asked

### How long does a passport take in South Africa?

Home Affairs aims for about two to three weeks, but allow four to six weeks to be safe, especially before December and the Easter holidays.

### Can I renew my passport at a bank?

Yes. Bank branches that offer Home Affairs services can process passport renewals. Book and pay online through eHomeAffairs first.

### How many blank pages do I need in my passport?

At least two completely blank pages for most trips, and more for trips that cross several borders.

### Do I need a passport to go to Lesotho?

Yes. Lesotho is a separate country, so every traveller needs a valid passport, including children.

### Which countries can South Africans visit without a visa?

Many, including most of our neighbours: Lesotho, Eswatini, Mozambique, Namibia, Botswana, Zimbabwe and Zambia, as well as Tanzania (Zanzibar) and Thailand for short holidays. Rules change, so always check before you book.
