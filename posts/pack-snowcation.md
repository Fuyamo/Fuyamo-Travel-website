A weekend in the snow is one of the best winter trips you can do without leaving southern Africa, but it catches a lot of first-timers out. Jeans that freeze, gloves that soak through and sunburn in July are all common. This packing list is built from what we've learned on our Lesotho Snowcation trips, and it works whether it's your first time in snow or your fifth.

## How cold is Afriski in winter?

Afriski Mountain Resort sits at around 3,000 metres in the Maluti Mountains of northern Lesotho, just below the Mahlasela Pass (3,222 m). That makes it one of the highest places you'll ever spend a weekend in southern Africa. The ski season usually runs from early June to late August. Snow machines keep the slopes covered, and natural snowfalls are a bonus.

What that means for packing:

- **It's cold.** On the mountain, daytime temperatures often hover around freezing, and nights drop well below zero.
- **It's windy.** Wind makes it feel much colder than the thermometer says.
- **It's wet.** Snow melts on your clothes, and you'll sit, fall and roll in it.
- **It's bright.** UV is stronger at altitude, and fresh snow reflects up to 80% of the sun's UV back at you. You can burn badly in July.

Our trip also starts in Clarens, in the Free State, where winter mornings are frosty but the days are often sunny and mild. So you're packing for two climates: a crisp country town and a snowy mountain.

## The secret: three layers, not one big jacket

The biggest mistake is wearing one huge jacket over a T-shirt. You'll be too hot on the bus, too cold on the slope and soaked by lunchtime. Dress in layers instead, so you can add or remove one as the day changes.

1. **Base layer:** a thermal top and thermal leggings that sit close to your skin. Choose wool or synthetic fabric. Avoid cotton, which holds sweat and makes you cold.
2. **Mid layer:** a fleece or warm jersey that traps heat.
3. **Outer layer:** a waterproof, windproof jacket and waterproof pants. This is what keeps the snow out.

Most sports and outdoor stores sell thermals and waterproof gear in winter, and you don't need expensive ski brands for one weekend. If you don't own ski pants, ask around: friends who've been to the snow often have a pair to lend.

## What to wear on the slopes

- **Waterproof pants.** This is the one item not to skip. Jeans and tracksuit pants soak through within minutes, then freeze.
- **Waterproof gloves.** Wet hands end a snow day fast. Thin glove liners underneath add warmth and let you use your phone.
- **Long, warm socks.** Wool or ski socks, one pair at a time. Two pairs squash your feet and cut circulation, which makes your toes colder, not warmer.
- **Beanie and neck warmer.** A buff or neck gaiter you can pull up over your face in the wind.
- **Goggles or proper sunglasses.** Look for UV400 protection. Snow glare is tiring on the eyes and can cause snow blindness.
- **Waterproof boots with grip** for walking around the resort. Ski or snowboard boots come with your hired gear.

Skis, snowboards, boots and helmets can all be hired at the resort, and Afriski has a ski school for beginners. Skiing and the other snow activities are optional extras on our trip, so you choose how you want to spend your day on the mountain.

## Sun and skin care for the snow

- Sunscreen with SPF 50. Reapply at lunch, and don't forget your ears and the back of your neck.
- Lip balm with SPF. Cold, dry air cracks lips quickly.
- A rich moisturiser for your face and hands.
- Tissues. The cold makes everyone's nose run.

## Clarens and the evenings

Clarens is where the adventure part of the trip happens, at Clarens Xtreme. Optional activities include quad biking, a zipline, paintball, archery and wall climbing, so pack:

- Comfortable pants you can move in and don't mind getting muddy
- Trainers or boots with grip
- A warm jacket for frosty mornings

Evenings are about getting warm. Pack comfortable clothes, a hoodie, warm socks or slippers, and something to sleep in that's warmer than you think you need.

## Altitude, water and energy

At around 3,000 metres you may feel out of breath sooner than usual, and some people get a mild headache. It usually passes. To help:

- Drink plenty of water, even though you won't feel thirsty in the cold.
- Take it slowly on your first walk up a slope.
- Go easy on alcohol on the first night.
- Pack snacks. Playing in the snow burns a lot of energy.

## Phones, photos and power

Cold drains phone batteries fast. Keep your phone in an inside pocket close to your body, and bring a charged power bank. A waterproof phone pouch is handy for photos in falling snow.

## Documents: yes, you need your passport

Lesotho is a separate country, so you'll cross an international border to reach Afriski.

- **Passport:** every traveller needs a valid passport, including children. Your South African ID card or ID book won't get you across.
- **Stamps:** you get four stamps in one weekend (out of South Africa, into Lesotho, and back again), so check you have blank pages.
- **Children:** South African children travelling across a border should carry an unabridged birth certificate. If only one parent is travelling, bring a consent affidavit from the other parent.
- **Money:** Lesotho's currency, the loti, is pegged to the rand, and rand is accepted everywhere. Bring some cash for extras, snacks and souvenirs.

Our [passport checklist for South Africans](/travel-talk/passport-checklist/) covers renewals, children's passports and how long it all takes.

## Packing for kids: the Family Edition list

Children get cold and wet faster than adults, and they'll spend far longer rolling in the snow. On top of the list above, pack:

- Two pairs of waterproof gloves per child (one to wear, one drying)
- An extra set of socks and a change of clothes in your day bag
- Children's sunscreen, lip balm and sunglasses or goggles
- Snacks and a refillable water bottle
- Something to keep them busy on the drive
- Name labels on everything. Every glove looks the same in the snow.

Our [Lesotho Snowcation Family Edition](/trips/lesothofam/) is built for parents travelling with kids, with separate prices for adults and children.

## What not to pack

- Jeans as your snow pants
- Cotton socks or a cotton hoodie as your top layer
- Smooth-soled sneakers (the resort paths get icy)
- Your best designer jacket. Snow, mud and paintball don't mix with it.
- Too many outfits. You'll live in your layers.

## The complete Afriski packing checklist

- Passport (and children's passports, birth certificates and affidavits if needed)
- Thermal top and leggings (2 sets)
- Fleece or warm jersey
- Waterproof, windproof jacket
- Waterproof ski pants
- Waterproof gloves and glove liners
- Wool or ski socks (one pair per day, plus a spare)
- Beanie and neck warmer
- Goggles or UV400 sunglasses
- Waterproof boots with grip
- Comfortable clothes and trainers for Clarens
- Warm evening clothes and slippers
- Sunscreen SPF 50, SPF lip balm and moisturiser
- Water bottle and snacks
- Power bank and charging cable
- Cash in rand
- Any medication you take, plus something for headaches

## Lesotho snow trip questions we get asked

### When is the ski season at Afriski?

Usually from early June to late August. July is the heart of the season. Our 2027 Snowcation departures are 16–18 July and 6–8 August, and the Family Edition is 2–4 July.

### Is there always snow at Afriski?

During the season, snow machines keep the slopes covered, so there's snow to play on even if it hasn't snowed naturally. Real snowfall on the mountains is a bonus.

### Do I need to know how to ski?

No. Many of our travellers have never skied. You can take a lesson at the ski school, or try tubing and bum boarding, which need no experience at all.

### Can I go to Lesotho with my South African ID?

No. You need a valid passport to cross into Lesotho, and so do children.
