## The Dubai stopover

Breaking the journey in Dubai turns a long trip into two holidays. Our stopover includes a Dubai desert experience: you head out of the city into the dunes, usually in a 4x4, for the kind of sunset you can only get in the desert. Quad biking and a desert jeep ride are optional extras if you want more adrenaline.

Dubai is very hot in June (often above 40°C) and pleasantly warm in December. Dress modestly in public places and malls: cover your shoulders and knees, and keep swimwear for the beach and the pool.

## Phuket: what the week looks like

Phuket is Thailand's largest island, on the Andaman Sea in the south-west of the country. It's a good base for a first trip to Thailand: beaches, island day trips, temples, food and nightlife, all within an hour or so of each other.

### Island hopping to Phi Phi or Maya Bay

The Phi Phi Islands are the postcard Thailand: sheer limestone cliffs, turquoise water and long-tail boats. Maya Bay, on Phi Phi Leh, became world-famous after the film The Beach. A few things to know:

- **Swimming isn't allowed in Maya Bay.** You can walk on the beach and wade in the shallows. Boats now drop visitors on the other side of the island, and you walk across.
- **Maya Bay closes every year** for August and September, so the reef and sharks can recover. Both of our departures fall outside the closure.
- **The sea decides.** In June, boat trips can be moved to a calmer day if the sea is rough.

Bring a dry bag, reef shoes, sunscreen and motion-sickness tablets if you need them.

### Elephant sanctuary

Thailand's ethical sanctuaries care for elephants rescued from logging, riding and tourist shows. Visits are about watching, feeding and learning, not riding. Wear clothes you don't mind getting muddy.

### The Big Buddha on Nakkerd Hill

The Big Buddha is a white marble statue about 45 metres tall on a hilltop in the south of the island, with views over Chalong Bay. It's a place of worship, so cover your shoulders and knees (sarongs are usually available at the entrance) and speak softly.

### Phuket Old Town

Phuket Town's old quarter is lined with pastel Sino-Portuguese shophouses built during the tin-mining boom. Walk Thalang Road and Soi Romanee for cafés, street art and photos. On Sunday evenings, Thalang Road becomes the Lard Yai walking street market, with food stalls, crafts and music.

Also included: a **Phuket Ancient City tour**. And if you want a party day, **Yona Beach Club** is one of the optional extras.

## Do South Africans need a visa for Thailand?

No, not for a short holiday. South African passport holders can visit Thailand visa-free for tourism. At the time of writing, the visa-free stay is 30 days (it was 60 days until September 2026), which is plenty for our trip. You'll need:

- A passport valid for at least six months from your arrival date.
- Proof of your onward or return flight.
- The **Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC)**. It's free and takes a few minutes to fill in on the official [Thai immigration website](https://tdac.immigration.go.th/) within the three days before you arrive. Lookalike sites charge a fee, so use only the official one.

## Thailand in June or December?

Phuket is hot all year, at about 27 to 32°C. The difference between our two departures is the rain and the sea.

- **June:** the start of the south-west monsoon on the Andaman coast. Expect showers, sometimes heavy, mixed with sunny spells. The island is green, quieter and better value. The sea is rougher, so obey the red flags on the beaches: rip currents are dangerous in monsoon season.
- **December:** peak season. Dry, sunny days, calm turquoise water and the best conditions for island trips. It's also the busiest time, so book activities early.

Both are good. June suits travellers who want quieter beaches and don't mind a passing storm. December is for guaranteed blue skies.

## Money in Thailand

- **Cash is still king** at markets, street food stalls and in tuk-tuks.
- **ATM fees add up.** Thai ATMs charge foreign cards a fee of around 220 baht per withdrawal, on top of your own bank's fees. Draw larger amounts less often.
- **Pay in baht.** When an ATM or card machine offers to charge you in rand, say no. The conversion rate is almost always worse.
- **Bargain politely** at markets, but not in shops with fixed prices.
- **Tipping** isn't expected, but rounding up or leaving 20 to 100 baht for good service is appreciated.

## Thai etiquette: what to know before you go

- **Respect the royal family.** Criticising the monarchy is a serious crime in Thailand. Stand for the royal anthem if it plays before a film.
- **Dress for temples.** Shoulders and knees covered, and shoes off before you go into a temple building.
- **Heads and feet.** Don't touch anyone's head, and don't point your feet at people or Buddha images.
- **Leave your vape at home.** E-cigarettes are illegal in Thailand, and tourists have been fined.
- **Smile.** Losing your temper in public is frowned on. A calm, friendly approach solves most problems.

## What to eat in Phuket

- **Massaman and southern Thai curries.** Southern food is spicy, rich and full of flavour.
- **Tom yum goong,** the hot and sour prawn soup.
- **Pad Thai** from a street stall.
- **Moo hong,** a slow-cooked Phuket pork belly stew.
- **Mee hokkien,** Phuket's thick yellow noodles.
- **Mango sticky rice** for dessert.

## Thailand questions we get asked

### How long is the flight from South Africa to Thailand?

There's no direct flight. Via Dubai, it's about 8 hours to Dubai and 6 and a half hours on to Phuket, plus the time in between.

### Do South Africans need a visa for Thailand?

No. South Africans can visit Thailand visa-free for tourism (30 days at the time of writing). You must fill in the free Thailand Digital Arrival Card online before you arrive.

### Is June a good time to visit Phuket?

Yes, if you don't mind some rain. June is quieter and greener, with showers and sunny spells. Boat trips can be rescheduled if the sea is rough.

### Can I go to Thailand on my own?

Yes. Many of our travellers join solo, and travelling with a group means company from the first flight. Ask us about room-sharing options.
