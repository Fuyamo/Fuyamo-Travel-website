## Nungwi or Stone Town? Why our trip does both

Choosing where to stay is the biggest Zanzibar question, and the answer depends on what you want from the week. Nungwi is for beach days, swimming and sunsets. Stone Town is for history, markets and food. One without the other feels like half a trip, so we split the week between them.

### Nungwi: beaches you can swim at any time of day (3 nights)

Nungwi sits on the northern tip of Unguja, Zanzibar's main island, and it has two big advantages. First, the tide here doesn't go out as far as it does on the east coast, so you can swim for most of the day. In places like Paje and Jambiani the sea can retreat hundreds of metres at low tide, which is great for kitesurfing but not for a swim. Second, Nungwi faces west, so the sun sets over the ocean every evening.

What's built into the Nungwi days:

- **Swimming with turtles.** A gentle, unforgettable first morning in the water.
- **A sunset dhow cruise.** Nungwi is known for its traditional wooden dhows, and evening sailings are the best way to see the sunset.
- **Island hopping to Prison Island and Nakupenda.** Prison Island (Changuu) is home to a colony of giant tortoises, some more than a century old. Nakupenda, which means "I love you" in Swahili, is a pure-white sandbank that appears in the middle of the sea. Bring a hat, because there's no shade.

### Stone Town: the old heart of Zanzibar (2 nights)

Stone Town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the cultural centre of the Swahili coast: a maze of narrow streets, carved wooden doors, mosques, markets and rooftop cafés. It's where Zanzibar's African, Arab, Indian and European histories meet, and it's best explored on foot.

Our historic landmarks tour shows you the town's story. Other highlights while you're there:

- **Forodhani Gardens night market.** Every evening the waterfront fills with grills. Try Zanzibar pizza, seafood skewers and fresh sugar-cane juice.
- **Darajani Market.** Spices, fruit, fish and everyday life. Go in the morning.
- **The Old Fort and the seafront.** A good spot for photos at sunset.
- **The former slave market site.** The Anglican cathedral stands on the site of the old slave market, with an exhibition telling the history of the East African slave trade. It's sobering and important.
- **Freddie Mercury's birthplace.** The Queen frontman was born in Stone Town in 1946.

Stone Town is a conservative, mostly Muslim community. Cover your shoulders and knees when you walk around town, and keep swimwear for the beach and the hotel.

## Do South Africans need a visa for Zanzibar?

No. Zanzibar is part of Tanzania, and South African passport holders can visit Tanzania on holiday without a visa for up to 90 days. You'll need:

- A passport valid for at least 6 months after your return date, with blank pages.
- Proof of your return flight and accommodation, so keep your trip documents handy.
- The compulsory Zanzibar inbound travel insurance certificate.

### Zanzibar's compulsory travel insurance

Since October 2024, every visitor to Zanzibar must have a travel insurance policy issued by the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation. Your own travel insurance doesn't replace it. At the time of writing it costs US$44 per adult, and the official portal also lists lower rates for children and for travellers from SADC countries like South Africa, so check your price when you apply.

Buy it only on the official portal, [inbound.visitzanzibar.go.tz](https://inbound.visitzanzibar.go.tz/), before you travel. Copycat websites charge more. Keep the certificate on your phone and print a copy.

### Do I need a yellow fever certificate?

Not if you fly directly from South Africa. South Africa isn't a yellow fever risk country, so travellers coming straight from home don't need a certificate. You'd need one if your flight stops for more than 12 hours in a country with yellow fever risk, such as Kenya or Ethiopia. Speak to a travel clinic about malaria, which is present at low levels on the islands, and pack insect repellent.

## Is September a good time to visit Zanzibar?

Yes, it's one of the best. June to October is the long dry season. In September you can expect sunny days around 28 to 30°C, warm sea water, low humidity and very little rain. It's after the European summer peak, so beaches and boats are less crowded.

For comparison, the long rains fall from about March to May, and the short rains in November. December to February is hot and humid but also popular.

## Money in Zanzibar: shillings, dollars or rand?

- **US dollars** are widely accepted for hotels, tours and activities. Bring clean, newer notes. Old, torn or marked notes are often refused.
- **Tanzanian shillings** are better for markets, small restaurants, taxis and tips. Draw them from bank ATMs in Stone Town or Nungwi.
- **Rand** isn't accepted, so change money before you go or use ATMs on arrival.
- **Cards** work at most hotels and bigger restaurants, but don't rely on them in markets or small shops.

These aren't included in the trip price, but they're the ones our travellers rave about:

## Zanzibar questions we get asked

### How long is the flight from Johannesburg to Zanzibar?

A direct flight takes about 4 hours. Zanzibar is one hour ahead of South Africa.

### Is Zanzibar safe for South African travellers?

Zanzibar is generally safe and friendly for visitors. Use normal common sense: don't walk alone on empty beaches at night, keep valuables in your room safe and book boat trips through trusted operators.

### Can I drink alcohol in Zanzibar?

Yes, at hotels, beach bars and restaurants that serve it. Drinking in public in Stone Town is frowned on, so keep it to licensed places.

### Which is better, Nungwi or Kendwa?

They're neighbours on the same northern coast, both with swimmable beaches and sunsets. Nungwi has more restaurants and a village feel. Kendwa, just south, is quieter. You can walk between them along the beach at low tide.
