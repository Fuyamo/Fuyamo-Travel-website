Namibia is where the desert runs straight into the Atlantic Ocean. It's a short flight from Johannesburg, it's on the same time as home, the rand works everywhere and South Africans don't need a visa. Yet it feels like another planet: huge dunes, foggy seaside towns, thousands of flamingos and one of the biggest seal colonies in the world. Here's what to expect on our Namibia group trip, and what to know before you go.

## Namibia at a glance

- **Trip length:** 5 nights, 6 days in Windhoek, Swakopmund and Walvis Bay.
- **Getting there:** about 2 hours' flight from Johannesburg. Flights and all transfers are included on our trip.
- **Time difference:** none. Namibia is on the same time as South Africa.
- **Visa:** none needed for South African passport holders on holiday.
- **Money:** the Namibian dollar, which is worth exactly one rand. Rand is accepted everywhere.
- **Plugs:** the same as South Africa.
- **Driving:** on the left, like home.

## Windhoek: the capital

Windhoek sits in the central highlands at about 1,650 metres, so the air is dry and the nights are cool. It's a small, easy-going capital, and our Windhoek city tour shows you the highlights:

- **Christuskirche,** the city's landmark Lutheran church, built in the early 1900s.
- **The Independence Memorial Museum,** which tells the story of Namibia's road to independence in 1990.
- **Katutura,** where you'll find kapana: strips of beef grilled over open fires and dipped in spicy salt, sold at the Single Quarters market. It's Windhoek's best street food.

From Windhoek, the road to the coast crosses about 350 km of open country and desert. Watch the landscape change from bush and mountains to empty gravel plains, and feel the temperature drop as you near the sea.

## Swakopmund: the seaside adventure town

Swakopmund is Namibia's favourite holiday town: German colonial buildings, palm-lined streets, a long jetty, good coffee and a cool sea breeze. It's also the country's adventure capital, wedged between the Atlantic and the dunes of the Namib Desert.

Included here:

- **Quad biking in the dunes.** Riding the dune belt between Swakopmund and Walvis Bay is a highlight of any trip to Namibia. Wear closed shoes and a buff to keep the sand out.
- **Camel riding.** A slow, swaying ride on the edge of the desert, and a great photo.

Mornings in Swakopmund are often grey and foggy, thanks to the cold Benguela current offshore. The fog usually burns off by late morning.

## Walvis Bay: Dune 7 and the flamingos

Walvis Bay is about 30 minutes south of Swakopmund. It has two big sights:

- **Dune 7.** One of the tallest dunes in Namibia, at roughly 380 metres. Climbing it is a workout, running down it is the reward, and the view across the desert is worth both.
- **The flamingo lagoon.** Walvis Bay's lagoon is a protected wetland, and it's home to thousands of greater and lesser flamingos that turn the shallows pink. Bring binoculars or a zoom lens.

If you have time and budget, ask about a 4x4 trip to **Sandwich Harbour**, where giant dunes fall straight into the ocean. It's not part of the trip, but it's one of the most spectacular drives in Africa.

## Cape Cross: the seal colony

Cape Cross, on the Skeleton Coast north of Swakopmund, is home to one of the largest Cape fur seal colonies in the world. Tens of thousands of seals crowd the rocks, and in the breeding season the numbers climb even higher. Expect the noise of thousands of barking seals and a smell you'll never forget. A walkway lets you get close without disturbing them.

Cape Cross is also where the Portuguese explorer Diogo Cão planted a stone cross in 1486, which is how the cape got its name. A replica stands there today.

## Do South Africans need a visa for Namibia?

No. South African passport holders can visit Namibia on holiday without a visa. What you do need:

- A passport valid for at least six months after your trip, with blank pages for the stamps.
- For children: their own passport, plus an unabridged birth certificate and, if only one parent is travelling, a consent affidavit from the other parent.

Our [passport checklist for South Africans](/travel-talk/passport-checklist/) covers renewals and children's documents in detail.

## What's the weather like in Namibia in May?

May is the start of Namibia's dry winter, and one of the best months to visit.

- **Inland (Windhoek):** sunny, warm days around the mid-20s °C, and cold nights that can drop below 10°C.
- **On the coast (Swakopmund and Walvis Bay):** cooler, around 18 to 22°C, with morning fog and wind in the afternoons.
- **Rain:** very little. It's the end of the rainy season inland and almost never rains on the coast.

The trick is layers: a T-shirt for the desert at midday, and a warm jacket for foggy mornings and evenings.

## Is there malaria in Windhoek and Swakopmund?

Windhoek, Swakopmund and Walvis Bay are not malaria areas. Malaria in Namibia is found in the far north and north-east. If you're adding a trip north, speak to a travel clinic.

## Money in Namibia: rand or Namibian dollars?

- **Rand** is accepted everywhere at the same value as the Namibian dollar.
- **Ask for change in rand** where you can. Namibian dollars can't be used in South Africa, so spend them before you fly home.
- **Cards** work in most shops, restaurants and hotels.
- Carry some cash for markets, kapana stands and tips.

## What to eat and drink in Namibia

- **Kapana** in Windhoek, with a fresh vetkoek.
- **Game meat:** oryx (gemsbok), kudu and springbok steaks are on most menus.
- **Walvis Bay oysters,** some of the best in the world, grown in the cold Atlantic.
- **Seafood in Swakopmund,** from kingklip to calamari.
- **German bakes:** apple strudel and Black Forest cake in Swakopmund's cafés.
- **Windhoek Lager,** brewed under the old German beer purity law.

## What to pack for Namibia

- Layers: T-shirts, a jersey or fleece, and a warm jacket
- A windbreaker for the coast
- Closed shoes for the dunes and quad biking
- A buff or scarf for sand and wind
- Sunscreen, sunglasses, a hat and lip balm (the air is very dry)
- Binoculars or a camera with a zoom for flamingos and seals
- Your passport and some cash

## Namibia questions we get asked

### How far is Namibia from Johannesburg?

About 2 hours by plane. Driving takes the best part of two days, which is why our trip flies.

### Do South Africans need a visa for Namibia?

No. South Africans can visit Namibia on holiday without a visa. You need a valid passport.

### Can I use rand in Namibia?

Yes. The Namibian dollar is pegged to the rand, and rand is accepted everywhere. Namibian dollars aren't accepted back home, so spend them before you leave.

### What's the difference between Swakopmund and Walvis Bay?

They're 30 minutes apart. Swakopmund is the holiday town, with cafés, shops and adventure activities. Walvis Bay is the port town, with the flamingo lagoon, Dune 7 and the oysters.
