## How the 7-day Bali itinerary flows

We split the week between the island's two personalities: the green, spiritual centre around Ubud, and the beach and café life of Canggu on the south-west coast. Uluwatu and Nusa Penida are day trips in between.

### Ubud: jungle, temples and the Bali Swing (3 nights)

Ubud is Bali's green heart. Rice terraces, jungle ravines, temples, and a town full of cafés, galleries and markets. It's the slower, cultural half of the trip and the best place to shake off the long flight.

Built into the Ubud days:

- **Ubud cultural tour.** Temples, local crafts and the stories behind the small offerings you'll see on every doorstep.
- **The Bali Swing.** You swing out over the jungle canopy, harnessed in. Wear something that moves in the wind (the flowing-dress photo is a Bali classic) and shoes that stay on.
- **A waterfall experience.** Bali's waterfalls are cool, green and loud. Wear shoes with grip, because wet rocks are slippery.
- **Cretya Ubud day pass.** Pools, loungers and rice-terrace views. Treat it as your recovery day.

### Nusa Penida day trip

Nusa Penida is a rugged island off Bali's south-east coast, reached by fast boat. It's famous for cliffs and viewpoints, and most day tours take in the west-coast highlights such as Kelingking Beach and its dinosaur-shaped headland.

Expect an early start, a boat crossing that can be choppy and bumpy roads on the island. If you get seasick, take something before you board. Bring cash for snacks and toilet stops, plus sunscreen and a hat, because there's little shade at the viewpoints.

### Uluwatu Temple at sunset

Pura Luhur Uluwatu sits on a clifftop about 70 metres above the Indian Ocean, on the Bukit Peninsula in the far south. Come for the sunset; many visitors stay on for the Kecak fire dance, which is performed there in the evening.

Two things to know. Cover your shoulders and knees (sarongs are handed out at the entrance). And keep sunglasses, hats and phones out of reach of the temple monkeys. They're fast, and they're known for trading stolen items back for food.

### Canggu: the beach half (2 nights)

The last two nights are about slowing down. Canggu is Bali's surf-and-café town: black-sand beaches, beach clubs, smoothie bowls and sunsets that turn the whole sky orange. Take a surf lesson, book a massage, shop, or do nothing at all. On the last day it's back to the airport, which is about an hour away depending on traffic.

## Do South Africans need a visa for Bali?

Yes, but it's quick. South African passport holders get Indonesia's **Visa on Arrival (VoA)**. You can buy it at the airport when you land, or online before you fly as an **e-VoA** on the official [Indonesian immigration website](https://evisa.imigrasi.go.id/), which lets you skip the payment queue.

- **Cost:** IDR 500,000 (about US$30).
- **Length of stay:** 30 days, which can be extended once for another 30.
- **Passport:** valid for at least 6 months from the day you arrive, with blank pages for the stamp.

Only use the official website. Copycat sites look official and charge far more for the same visa.

Two more things to sort out before you land:

- **Bali tourist levy:** IDR 150,000 (about US$10) per person, paid once per visit. Pay online through the official Love Bali website or app before you fly, and keep the QR code on your phone.
- **All Indonesia arrival card:** a free online form that combines immigration, customs and health questions. Fill it in within 3 days before you land, and show the QR code at the airport.

The visa, levy and arrival card aren't part of the trip price, so budget about US$40 per person for them. Entry rules change, so check again close to departure. Stuck on a form? ## When is the best time to visit Bali?

Bali has two seasons. The dry season runs from about April to October, and the wet season from about November to March. Temperatures barely change: expect warm, humid days around 26 to 31°C all year.

Our two 2027 departures sit either side of the dry season, and both are good times to go:

- **March:** the tail end of the wet season. The island is at its greenest, the crowds are thinner, and rain tends to fall in short, heavy bursts in the afternoon before clearing. Nyepi, Bali's Day of Silence, when the whole island (airport included) shuts down for 24 hours, falls in early March 2027, before our trip.
- **November:** the start of the wet season. Mornings are usually clear and showers come and go, mostly later in the day. It's also before the December holiday rush.

Pack a light rain jacket for either trip, and plan beach time for the mornings.

## Is Bali expensive for South Africans?

The rand goes reasonably far in Bali. A plate of nasi goreng or mie goreng at a warung (a small, family-run restaurant) costs a fraction of a restaurant meal at home. Beach clubs and Western-style cafés in Canggu, on the other hand, charge close to Joburg or Cape Town prices, and sometimes more.

Ways to make your money go further:

- **Eat local at least once a day.** Warungs are where the best food is anyway.
- **Use bank ATMs**, ideally inside a bank or mall. When the machine or card terminal offers to charge you in rand, say no and pay in rupiah. The conversion rate is almost always worse.
- **Carry small notes.** Market and driver prices are often negotiable, and change can be scarce.
- **Budget for the extras:** the visa and levy, optional activities, spa treatments, souvenirs and travel insurance.

On our trip, your flights, accommodation with breakfast, transfers and the main activities are already paid for, so your spending money mostly goes on lunches, dinners, drinks and shopping.

## First-timer tips for Bali

- **Dress for temples.** Shoulders and knees covered. A sarong in your day bag solves this everywhere.
- **Watch the water.** Drink bottled or filtered water, not tap water, and be careful with ice from street stalls. Bali belly is real.
- **Mind the offerings.** The small palm-leaf baskets (canang sari) on pavements and doorsteps are daily offerings. Step around them.
- **Use your right hand** to give and receive things, and don't touch people's heads.
- **Sun and mosquitoes.** Pack high-SPF sunscreen and an insect repellent with DEET.
- **Get connected early.** Buy an eSIM before you fly or a local SIM at the airport, so maps and WhatsApp work from the start.
- **Get travel insurance** that includes medical cover, and check it covers any adventure activities you plan to do.

## Bali questions we get asked

### How long is the flight from South Africa to Bali?

There's no direct flight. With one connection, expect roughly 15 to 20 hours of total travel from Johannesburg, depending on the route and the layover.

### Can I go to Bali on my own?

Yes. Many of our travellers join solo, and on a group trip you have company from day one. Ask us about room-sharing options.

### What should I pack for Bali?

Light, breathable clothes, a sarong or scarf for temples, swimwear, sandals plus shoes with grip, a light rain jacket, sunscreen, insect repellent and a plug adapter.

### Is Bali safe for tourists?

Bali is generally relaxed and welcoming. Use the same common sense you would at home: keep valuables out of sight, use registered transport and take care on the roads, which are busy with scooters.
