## About the falls: the Smoke that Thunders

The local name for Victoria Falls is Mosi-oa-Tunya, "the smoke that thunders". The Zambezi River drops about 108 metres into a narrow gorge along a front of roughly 1.7 kilometres, making it one of the largest curtains of falling water on Earth. The falls form the border between Zimbabwe and Zambia, and they're a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The Scottish explorer David Livingstone was the first European to see them, in 1855, and named them after Queen Victoria. The town of Livingstone, on the Zambian side, is named after him.

## Victoria Falls: Zimbabwe side or Zambia side?

It's the most common question, and the honest answer is that both are worth seeing. That's why our trip does both.

### The Zimbabwe side

Most of the falls face Zimbabwe, so this is where you get the classic, wide views. A path runs through the rainforest along the edge of the gorge, with a series of viewpoints looking straight across at the Main Falls, Horseshoe Falls and Danger Point. Even at low water, the Main Falls keep flowing. Our day visit to Victoria Falls is on this side.

### The Zambia side

From Zambia you're closer to the water and can look down into the gorge from the Knife-Edge Bridge, or climb down to the Boiling Pot, where the river churns at the bottom. Livingstone Island, home of Devil's Pool, is on this side too. Our day trip to Livingstone takes you across.

You cross between the two countries on the **Victoria Falls Bridge**, built in 1905. It's a border crossing, so you'll be stamped out of Zimbabwe and into Zambia, and back again. It's also where the famous 111-metre bungee jump is, if you're brave enough.

## Devil's Pool: swimming on the edge of the falls

Devil's Pool is a natural rock pool right on the lip of the falls on the Zambian side. When the river is low, a rock wall under the water stops you from going over, so you can swim to the edge and look straight down. It's only open in the low-water season, usually from around mid-August to December, and October and November are the most reliable months.

Our October departure is right in that window. It's an optional extra, visits are by guided tour only and numbers are strictly limited, so tell us early if you want to go.

## A day in Chobe, Botswana

Chobe National Park in northern Botswana is about 80 km from Victoria Falls, across the border at Kazungula. It's famous for having one of the largest elephant populations in Africa, and in the dry season herds gather along the Chobe River to drink and swim. Hippos, crocodiles, buffalo and a huge variety of birds are almost guaranteed.

Our day trip to Chobe is included, and a Chobe game drive is available as an optional extra. October is one of the best months of the year for game viewing there, because the bush is dry and the animals stay close to the water.

## Do South Africans need a visa for Victoria Falls?

No. South African passport holders don't need a visa for Zimbabwe, Zambia or Botswana for a holiday. The KAZA UniVisa you may read about is for nationalities that do need visas, so it doesn't apply to South Africans.

What you do need:

- **A passport** valid for at least six months after your trip.
- **Plenty of blank pages.** Crossing into Zambia and Botswana and back means a lot of stamps in one week. Make sure you have at least three or four empty pages.
- **For children:** their own passport, an unabridged birth certificate and, if only one parent is travelling, a consent affidavit from the other parent.

Read our [passport checklist for South Africans](/travel-talk/passport-checklist/) for the details.

## Is October a good time to visit Victoria Falls?

Yes, with one thing to know. October is the end of the dry season, and:

- **It's hot.** Days are often in the mid-30s °C. Plan active things for the mornings.
- **The water is low.** The falls are at their smallest, and parts of the Zambian side can be almost dry rock. The Zimbabwe side still flows, and with less spray you can actually see the gorge and the rock face clearly.
- **Devil's Pool is open,** which it isn't in high-water months.
- **Game viewing in Chobe is at its best.**

For comparison, the falls are at full flood from about March to May. The spray then is so heavy you'll be soaked and may not see much at all.

## Money at Victoria Falls

- **Bring US dollars** in small notes. They're the easiest currency for tips, markets, taxis and extras in all three countries.
- **Cards** work at most hotels, restaurants and activity companies in Victoria Falls town and Livingstone.
- **Zimbabwe** uses US dollars for most tourist spending, alongside its own currency.
- **Zambia** uses the kwacha, but tourist businesses usually accept dollars.
- **Botswana** uses the pula. On a Chobe day trip, you'll rarely need it.

## Is there malaria at Victoria Falls?

Yes. Victoria Falls, Livingstone and Chobe are all in malaria areas. See a travel clinic or your GP a few weeks before you travel to ask about antimalarial tablets, use an insect repellent with DEET, and cover up in the evenings. October is in the dry season, when mosquitoes are fewer, but the risk is still there.
